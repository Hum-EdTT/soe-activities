CMS Scenario: Managing Escalation

Work through a classroom management scenario one stage at a time. Reflect on each stage as the situation unfolds.

Stage 1 of 5Introduction & Context

Setting the Scene

Objective: Help students understand the importance of calm, respectful classroom management.

Context

This scenario asks you to apply the CMS Bumps Model — a framework for keeping classroom responses proportionate to behaviour. The goal is to keep responses as low-key as possible, only "bumping up" when the existing strategy is no longer effective.

Before working through the scenario, review the overview of the CMS Bumps Model:

CMS Bumps Model Overview (PowerPoint) Opens at Slide 7 in a new tab.

Why this matters

In real classroom situations, the way you respond to early behaviours sets the tone for what comes next. Jumping straight to high-level responses can escalate a situation that could otherwise have been managed quietly. Working through this scenario will help you see how each "bump" works in practice.

Reflective Question

Why do you think staying calm is the first step in managing challenging behaviour?

Stage 2 of 5The Scenario

The Hat Refusal

Three roles play out the opening of the scenario. Pay attention to each character's goal and behaviour.

The Setup

A group of primary students have just returned from lunch. One of them is wearing a hat. The teacher asks the student to remove the hat when they sit at their desk.

The Roles

Teacher

Goal: Address the student's refusal to remove their hat in a respectful and effective manner.

Behaviour Snapshot

  • Initially underprepared and unsure of strategy.
  • May raise voice or use sarcasm when frustrated.
  • Focus drifts from behaviour to personal confrontation.

Student

Goal: Keep the hat at any cost and avoid looking bad in front of friends.

Behaviour Snapshot

  • Rolls eyes when approached.
  • Argues every time the teacher asks to remove the hat.
  • Attempts to recruit allies for support.

Allies

Goal: Support the student and help them keep the hat.

Behaviour Snapshot

  • Giggles and makes side comments.
  • Encourages student with phrases like "yeah!" or "That's unfair!"
  • Reacts dramatically when tension rises ("oooh!" or "Someone's in trouble").

Reflective Question

If you were the student, how would you feel about being asked to remove your hat? Why might allies join in?

Stage 3 of 5Escalation

Tension Rises

The situation escalates as the student refuses to give the hat back. Each character's behaviour intensifies.

How each character behaves

Teacher

  • Becomes visibly tense, body language stiffens.
  • May try to assert authority by raising voice or insisting on compliance.

Student

  • Escalates defiance, uses sarcasm or dismissive gestures.
  • Leverages peer support to maintain status.

Allies

  • Amplify disruption with laughter and verbal support.
  • Create a "crowd effect" that pressures the teacher further.

Reflective Question

What behaviours escalated the situation? Could these have been avoided? Why or why not?

Stage 4 of 5Breaking Point

The Situation Erupts

The scenario reaches its peak. The teacher must find a way to regain calm without further escalation.

What happens

The student takes the hat off, throws it on the floor, and storms out of the classroom. The allies are laughing and jeering, while the teacher stands in shock.

Behaviour Snapshot

Teacher

  • Frozen, unsure how to respond, emotional control tested.

Student

  • Displays dramatic exit to maintain peer status, escalates to full defiance.

Allies

  • Heighten chaos with laughter and mocking comments, reinforcing the disruption.

Reflective Question

At this breaking point, which CMS strategies could help the teacher regain calm and control without escalating further?

Stage 5 of 5Debrief & Analysis

Making Sense of It

Reflect on the full scenario, link it back to CMS principles, and plan how you'd approach it differently.

Discussion Points

  • How did each character feel throughout the scenario?
  • Teacher's escalation triggers — what set off the teacher's heightened response?
  • Student's tactics — what behaviours did the student use to maintain status?
  • Allies' role — how did the allies amplify the situation?
  • Link the dynamics back to the CMS Bumps principles you reviewed in Stage 1.

Reflective Question 1

Which CMS step was most difficult to apply in this scenario? Why?

Reflective Question 2

What will you do differently in your next classroom challenge?
