Direct Instruction

A teacher-centered approach where information is explicitly taught through lectures, demonstrations, and guided practice. The teacher clearly explains concepts and skills in a structured, sequential manner.

Expository Teaching

A method where teachers present information in an organized, systematic way. Content is delivered through explanations, descriptions, and presentations to help students understand new concepts.

Gradual Release Model

A structured approach following 'I do, We do, You do' - the teacher demonstrates, then guides students through practice together, and finally students work independently to apply their learning.

Scaffolding

Providing temporary support structures that help students reach higher levels of understanding. Support is gradually removed as students become more competent and confident.

Demonstration

Showing students how to perform a task or skill by modeling the process step-by-step. This visual and practical approach helps students see exactly what is expected.

Modelling

The teacher performs a task while thinking aloud, making their thought processes visible to students. This helps students understand not just what to do, but how to think through problems.

Lecture

A formal presentation of information where the teacher verbally delivers content to students. Most effective when combined with visual aids and opportunities for questions.

Explanation

Clarifying concepts, processes, or ideas by breaking them down into understandable parts. Good explanations use examples, analogies, and clear language.

Guided Learning

The teacher provides direction and support while students work through tasks. Questions and prompts help students discover solutions with appropriate assistance.

Shared Learning Intentions

Clearly communicating what students will learn and how they'll know they've succeeded. Learning goals and success criteria are made explicit and understood by all students.

Collaborative Strategies

Student-centred approaches

Think Pair Share

Students think individually about a question, discuss their thoughts with a partner, then share ideas with the larger group. This promotes reflection and peer learning.

Think, Write, Pair, Share

An extension of Think Pair Share where students write down their thoughts before discussing. This ensures all students formulate ideas and provides a record of thinking.

Round Robin

Students take turns sharing ideas or responses in a circular pattern within small groups. This ensures equal participation and gives everyone a voice.

Mix Pair Share

Students move around the room and pair up with different classmates to discuss questions or share ideas. This promotes interaction with diverse perspectives.

Inside Outside Circle

Students form two concentric circles facing each other. They discuss topics with their partner, then one circle rotates to create new partnerships for continued discussion.

Corners

Students move to different corners of the room based on their opinions, preferences, or answers. This creates visible groupings for discussion and debate.

Group Problem Solving

Students work together in teams to analyze and solve complex problems. This develops critical thinking, communication, and collaborative skills.

Peer Tutoring

Students teach and support each other's learning. More knowledgeable students help peers, reinforcing their own understanding while assisting others.

Reciprocal Teaching

Students take turns being the teacher, leading discussions and asking questions about the content. This develops comprehension and leadership skills.

Cooperative Learning

Structured group work where students are interdependent and individually accountable. Success depends on all members contributing and learning together.
